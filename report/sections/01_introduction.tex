\section{Introduction}

\textbf{Domain.} Cone penetration tests (CPTs) push an instrumented cone into the ground and record the cone
resistance \qc{} (in MPa) with depth: higher \qc{} means stiffer ground, and low-\qc{} layers indicate soft
material that matters for design. CPTs are the main source of shallow ground information on civil projects,
but each test samples only one vertical line, so the ground between tests is inferred. A conventional
investigation fixes the number and positions of CPTs in advance and completes them all; some add little
new information, while extra tests are usually ordered by judgement.

\textbf{Motivation and objective.} We ask: \emph{with only a few CPTs measured, can an AI model predict the
unmeasured profiles well enough to choose the next most useful CPTs and to stop once quality is adequate --
and what is that worth?} Fewer CPTs reduce direct cost, and finishing earlier can bring a whole programme
forward. The objectives are to (i)~compare five AI predictors under identical information; (ii)~wrap the
selected predictor in an adaptive selection and online stopping loop; (iii)~audit every stop against hidden
CPTs that the loop never sees; and (iv)~translate the resulting CPT counts into an owner's financial
scenario.

\textbf{Contribution.} The project provides a replay framework that evaluates prediction, location choice,
stopping and economic value together under one information boundary (\cref{fig:workflow}). It reports
negative results as well as savings.

\textbf{Scope.} We use public CPT data in the 2--8\,m depth window. The threshold $\qc<1$\,MPa is a research
indicator of soft ground, not a soil-classification, bearing-capacity or liquefaction criterion. The
economics are counterfactual scenarios, not observed contract savings.
