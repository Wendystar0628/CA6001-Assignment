\textbf{Summary.}
Cone penetration tests (CPTs) measure ground resistance along single vertical lines, so the ground between
tests must be inferred. We ask whether AI models can predict unmeasured CPT profiles from a few measured
ones, choose where to test next and decide when to stop, and what that is worth. Five predictors -- a
Gaussian process (GP), XGBoost, a conditional neural process (CNP) and two TabPFN hybrids -- are replayed
on eight sites. Stopping thresholds were frozen on four development sites, and every stop was audited
against hidden CPTs. The TabPFN hybrids gave the lowest profile error, but within about 3\% of XGBoost and
GP. On the four evaluation sites no early stop failed the audit (7 early stops in 24 runs). However, CPTs
were saved only at the most uniform site (21\,$\rightarrow$\,11, the minimum the rule allows). Adaptive
location choice showed no detectable advantage over a space-filling baseline. In the economic scenario,
time effects outweighed CPT prices: the net owner benefit was $+$\eur{23,300} at that site and negative
elsewhere.
