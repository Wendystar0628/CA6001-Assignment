\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Evaluation design}
The loop is replayed on historical data in a three-level, site-held-out design:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Source training}: models are trained on source sites in three folds, with checkpoints chosen on
  validation sites.
\item \emph{Development}: four replay sites select the model, acquisition weights and stop thresholds.
\item \emph{Evaluation}: four replay sites (Wellington Airport, Christchurch Stadium, Hollywood, New Lock)
  test the frozen configuration, each with fold weights that excluded it.
\end{itemize}
For each site and seed (17, 29, 43), whole groups are assigned to $A$ ($\approx$20\%), a dispersed $S_0$
($\geq$5 positions) and $C$. Three policies share each split:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Full plan} (\Bfull) completes all of $S_0\cup C$.
\item \textbf{Sequential} (\Bseq) adds the two candidates farthest from any revealed CPT, a space-filling
  \emph{maximin} baseline.
\item \textbf{Adaptive} (\Padapt) adds the two candidates with the highest acquisition score.
\end{itemize}
Both Sequential and Adaptive use the same stop rule. Comparing them isolates the value of location choice
from that of stopping early. Evaluation gives $4\times3\times2=24$ trajectories plus 12 full-plan runs.

\textbf{Metrics} (all on $A$):
\begin{itemize}
\item log-MAE, the mean $|\hat y-y|$;
\item thickness MAE, the mean $|\hat H-H|$;
\item low-cell precision, recall, F1 and FPR.
\end{itemize}
Averages are taken within seed, then site, then equally across sites.

\subsection{Acquisition score and stop rule}
For each candidate group the adaptive policy computes three signals:
\begin{itemize}
\item $U$, the mean GP standard deviation;
\item $D$, the share of cells where the primary model and XGBoost disagree on low cells;
\item $d$, the distance to the nearest revealed CPT.
\end{itemize}
It then ranks each signal within the pool and scores
\begin{equation}
a(x)=0.5\,\mathrm{rank}(U)+0.3\,\mathrm{rank}(D)+0.2\,\mathrm{rank}(d).
\end{equation}
The weights put uncertainty first and use spacing as a tie-breaker. The one alternative tried, which drops
$D$ (0.7, 0, 0.3), lost on development.

Each round the controller computes four signals over all planned positions:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Delta Q_{90}$, the 90th percentile of the change in predicted $\ln\qc$;
\item $\Delta H_{90}$, the same for thickness;
\item a leave-group-out log-MAE on revealed CPTs;
\item the 90th percentile of the GP standard deviation.
\end{itemize}
It stops when all four pass for two consecutive rounds, after at least nine CPTs and full quadrant
coverage. The earliest possible stop is therefore 11 CPTs. Thresholds are the 0.50 quantile of each signal
at acceptable development states, chosen to maximise verified saving minus false-stop rate; the 0.75 and 0.90
quantiles gave 17\% and 58\% false stops. A stop \emph{passes the audit} if log-MAE $\le0.626$, thickness MAE
$\le0.881$\,m and neither is worse than the full plan by more than 0.05 or 0.25\,m. Low-cell recall
$\ge0.5$ with FPR $\le0.1$ is a separate diagnostic.

\subsection{Economic model and tools}
The owner's net benefit relative to the full plan is
\begin{equation}
B_{\text{net}}=(C_{\text{full}}-C_{\text{policy}})+\alpha\,\Delta T_{\text{inv}}\,(c_{\text{day}}+b_{\text{day}})-C_{\text{AI}}.
\end{equation}
The terms are:
\begin{itemize}
\item $C$: \eur{750} per CPT;
\item $\Delta T_{\text{inv}}$: calendar days saved, at 5 CPTs per rig-day plus a 0.25-day decision delay per
  batch;
\item $c_{\text{day}}=\eur{5,000}$ avoidable project cost per day;
\item $b_{\text{day}}=\eur{3,000}$ earlier-opening benefit per day;
\item $C_{\text{AI}}=\eur{600}$.
\end{itemize}
The main scenario sets $\alpha=1$ (every day saved shortens the project); 0.5 and 0.75 are sensitivities.
Only the remobilisation and review rates come from a 2025 Dutch quotation \parencite{mos2025quote}; the rest
are stated assumptions \parencite{gao2015schedule,gao2020cost}, applied to every site.

Tools: Python 3.11, scikit-learn 1.9 \parencite{pedregosa2011sklearn}, XGBoost 3.2, PyTorch 2.11,
TabPFN 9.2.0 \parencite{priorlabs_tabpfn} and matplotlib, on an RTX 4080 GPU.
