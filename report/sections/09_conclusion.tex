\section{Conclusion}

We built and audited an end-to-end AI-guided CPT investigation loop combining five predictors, an
uncertainty--disagreement--distance acquisition score, a multi-signal stop rule and an economic model. The
TabPFN hybrids predicted best, but only by about 3\%. The frozen stop rule never stopped wrongly, yet it
saved CPTs only at the most uniform site (47.6\%, $+$\eur{23,300} in the main scenario), and adaptive
selection did not beat maximin. The main lesson is that an AI algorithm should be judged by the decision it
supports, with an independent audit and honest negative results. Here, site predictability and decision
latency mattered more than predictor accuracy.
