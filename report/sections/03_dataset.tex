\section{Dataset Description}

\textbf{Source.} The data are the public ISSMGE TC304 CPT databases \parencite{tc304}. They comprise the
Global-CPT collection (1,196 soundings from 59 sites; \cite{ching2023global}) and the 98 soundings of the
New Lock Terneuzen ship lock, operational since August 2025 \parencite{rws2025terneuzen}. Screening leaves
492 usable positions out of 503 raw profiles (495 distinct positions), forming 456 near-duplicate groups at
31 sites. That gives 11,808 depth cells, which are repeated measurements within CPTs, not independent
samples. Of the 31 sites, 23 \emph{source} sites only train the models. The 8 \emph{replay} sites, those
with at least 20 usable positions, are split into 4 development and 4 evaluation sites.

\textbf{Features and preprocessing.} Each record holds site, local coordinates (m), group, depth and \qc.
Raw curves are interpolated to the 24 cell mid-points and \qc{} is log-transformed. At New Lock, 80 of the
98 files cover the full window. The models' neighbour features are rebuilt from revealed CPTs only, at every
round: distances, local medians, low quantiles, low-cell fractions and vertical gradients.

\textbf{Exploratory analysis.} The 0.25\,m grid loses some thin layers: its thickness MAE against the raw
curves is 0.092\,m. Sites differ sharply (\cref{fig:data}). Wellington Airport has no low cells, while New
Lock has 24\% and strong lateral variability. Across source sites only about 8\% of cells are low. Three
properties of the data guided the algorithm choice: \emph{few CPTs per site}, \emph{spatial correlation}
and \emph{rare low cells}.
