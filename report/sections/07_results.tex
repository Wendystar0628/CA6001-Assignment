\textbf{Comparison.} TabPFN joint has the lowest evaluation profile error, 0.497 (\cref{tab:prediction}).
That is 2.9\% below XGBoost; TabPFN + graph is 1.8\% below. The margins are small: joint beats XGBoost in 9
of 12 site--seed cells, and at New Lock the GP is better than both hybrids (0.976 against 1.034). The GP has
the lowest evaluation thickness error (0.400\,m), mainly because it rarely predicts low cells (recall 14\%).
The graph union gives the highest recall (36\%) at the highest FPR (9.6\%). CNP never predicts a low cell.
Site difficulty dominates (\cref{fig:models}): log-MAE is 0.11--0.24 at Wellington Airport but 0.98--1.14 at
New Lock.

\textbf{Ablation and error analysis.} Fine-tuning improved the raw residual on development sites (0.627
$\rightarrow$ 0.598). On evaluation sites, however, pretrained and fine-tuned joint models differ by under
0.002, and most of the thickness gain comes from the spatial blend (0.626 $\rightarrow$ 0.483\,m). The
improvement therefore belongs to the hybrid, not to TabPFN alone. At New Lock, error peaks at 4--6\,m,
where 23\% of cells are low: the primary model finds 53\% of them, with 85 false alarms, while the GP flags
only 1--3\% of cells against 9--23\% true.

The adaptive score samples differently from maximin (\cref{fig:maps}). At New Lock it concentrates on the
variable central cluster, whereas maximin spreads out. At the three easier sites audit quality is inside the
targets from the first few CPTs (\cref{fig:quality}). At New Lock it plateaus above both targets, even with
all 64 CPTs.

\textbf{Stopping outcomes.} No early stop failed the audit (\cref{tab:decisions}), but the evidence is thin.
Only 7 of 24 evaluation runs stopped early: 6 at Wellington Airport, where every state passes, and one at
Hollywood. The rule's real value is that it never stopped at New Lock, where any early stop would have
failed. By contrast, a naive stop at the ninth CPT makes 7 false stops. The leave-group-out support signal
carries this safety: without it, 2--3 false stops appear.

\textbf{Savings.} Equal-site savings were 13.6\% (Sequential) and 11.9\% (Adaptive). Both come almost
entirely from Wellington Airport (21\,$\rightarrow$\,11 CPTs), which is the earliest stop the rule allows.
Adaptive selection showed no detectable advantage: across all 24 site--seed pairs, its stopping count
differed from Sequential only 3 times, in both directions.

\textbf{A conservative rule.} With hindsight, Christchurch Stadium met the targets with 9 of 18 CPTs and
Hollywood with 13--14 of 20, yet both adaptive runs went to the full plan. Several signals block jointly.
$\Delta H_{90}\le0$ still allows 10\% of positions to change, and its zero value is a quantile tie, not a
designed tolerance. GP uncertainty tracked audit error only weakly (Spearman $\rho=0.21$ against 0.60 on
development sites). Low-cell targets failed at all three sites with low cells, and audit sets are small
(5--16 positions).

\subsection{Economic value}

\begin{figure}[!htb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.86\linewidth]{fig07_economics.pdf}
\caption{Owner's financial scenario, adaptive policy (mean of three seeds). (a)~Components at $\alpha=1$.
(b)~Sensitivity to $\alpha$ and the daily opening benefit. (c)~Sensitivity to the decision delay per batch.}
\label{fig:econ}
\end{figure}

At Wellington Airport the adaptive policy yields $+$\eur{23,300} (\cref{fig:econ}). This comprises:
\begin{itemize}
\item \eur{7,500} from ten avoided CPTs;
\item \eur{16,400} from finishing 2.05 days earlier;
\item minus \eur{600} of AI cost.
\end{itemize}
Sequential earns the same, so the gain comes from staged stopping, not from AI location choice.

The other sites lose \eur{11,900}--\eur{55,900} despite using no extra CPTs, because each batch adds a 0.25-day
decision delay (6.9 days over 27--29 transitions at New Lock). With zero delay, those losses shrink to the
\eur{600} AI cost. With a one-day delay, New Lock would lose \eur{222,000}. The four-site mean is
$-$\eur{14,300} (Adaptive) and $-$\eur{13,500} (Sequential). No site changes sign within any tested
sensitivity range.
